BUTTER & LEMON-PEPPER WALLEYE
Open foil boat / gas grill

The simple butter-and-lemon-pepper cook you remember: tender walleye in a
shallow, open-topped foil boat, with the grill lid closed. No flipping,
breading, mustard binder, or complicated rub.

AT A GLANCE
Serves: about 2
Fish: 1 lb (454 g) walleye fillets, skinless or skin-on
Prep: about 10 minutes, plus thawing if needed
Grill ambient: 375-400 F (191-204 C), measured beside the fish at grate level
Internal finish: 145 F (63 C) in the thickest part of EVERY fillet
Heat: indirect; place the boat over an unlit burner zone
Boat: open top; grill lid: closed between checks

Cook estimates by the thickest part of a fillet:
  1/4-1/2 inch (6-13 mm):   roughly 8-14 minutes; first check at 6 minutes
  1/2-3/4 inch (13-19 mm):  roughly 12-20 minutes; first check at 8 minutes
  3/4-1 inch (19-25 mm):   roughly 16-25 minutes; first check at 12 minutes

These are planning estimates for this adaptation, not kitchen-tested
guarantees. Your grill, starting fish temperature, thickness, and boat
placement affect the time. The thermometer decides when it is done.

INGREDIENTS -- FOR 1 LB / 454 G OF FISH
- 1 lb walleye fillets, fully thawed and checked for pin bones
- 2 tablespoons (28 g) unsalted butter
- 1 teaspoon lemon-pepper seasoning
- 1 teaspoon fresh lemon juice, optional, for finishing on the plate
- ONLY if your lemon pepper is salt-free: 1/8 teaspoon fine salt

Check the seasoning label. If it already contains salt, omit the extra salt.
Blend strength varies; start with the listed quantity and adjust future
batches to taste. No garnish is required.

SCALING
Multiply ingredient amounts by your fish weight in pounds, or by its weight
in grams divided by 454. Do NOT multiply cooking time by total weight.

Fish weight        Unsalted butter    Lemon pepper    Optional lemon juice
1/2 lb / 227 g      1 tablespoon       1/2 teaspoon    1/2 teaspoon
1 lb / 454 g        2 tablespoons      1 teaspoon      1 teaspoon
2 lb / 907 g        4 tablespoons      2 teaspoons     2 teaspoons

For salt-free seasoning only, scale the separate salt the same way.
Use more small boats for a larger batch; do not stack or crowd the fillets.

EQUIPMENT
Heavy-duty aluminum foil; instant-read thermometer with a thin probe;
grate-level air thermometer; wide spatula; rimmed tray; heat-resistant gloves.

METHOD

1. PREP THE FISH
Thaw in the refrigerator and follow the package instructions. For vacuum-
packed fish, follow its directions about removing the packaging before
thawing. Pat dry, check carefully for bones, and keep cold until ready to
cook. Skin can stay on; place it down against the foil.

2. BUILD THE BOAT
Double a sheet of heavy-duty foil. Fold the sides upward about 1-2 inches
and crimp the corners tightly to hold melted butter. Keep a flat bottom
and leave the top open. Lightly grease the bottom with a little of the
measured butter. Lay the fish in one layer. Fold a very thin tail under
itself so it is less likely to overcook.

3. SEASON SIMPLY
Sprinkle the measured lemon pepper evenly over the fish. Add the separate
salt only if the blend is salt-free. Dot the remaining butter over the
fillets. Save the fresh lemon juice for after cooking.

4. SET UP YOUR GAS GRILL
You choose which burners are lit; their positions are not prescribed.
Follow your grill's lighting instructions, preheat, and clean the grates.
Keep at least one heat source lit and leave an unlit area large enough
for the boat. Adjust the lit burners until the air beside the fish holds
375-400 F (191-204 C). A lid thermometer may not match grate-level heat.
Never cover the entire grate with foil or obstruct the grill's vents.

5. GRILL WITH THE BOAT OPEN AND THE LID CLOSED
Set the boat over the unlit area. Close the grill lid and begin checking
at the time suggested for your fillet thickness. No flipping is needed.
Rotate the boat if one side cooks faster. Spoon a little melted butter
over the top if desired. If the butter is browning hard or drying out,
move the boat farther from direct heat and check the ambient temperature.

6. PROBE AND SERVE
Insert the probe sideways into the thickest part of each fillet without
touching the foil. Cook every fillet to 145 F (63 C) before removing it.
Easy flaking is a clue, not a replacement for a thermometer.
Support the boat with a wide spatula and slide it onto a rimmed tray.
Use heat protection and watch for spilling butter. Let settle briefly
for handling, then lift the fish onto plates. Spoon the butter over it
and add the optional fresh lemon juice. There is no required three-minute
rest for fish at this internal-temperature target.

WHY IT WORKS
- The foil acts as a small pan: it supports fragile fish and keeps the
  butter and cooking juices around it. An open boat allows steam to escape;
  sealing it into a packet changes the cooking environment.
- Fish has short muscle fibers and delicate connective tissue. It flakes
  readily as it cooks and does not benefit from a long tenderizing cook.
  Extended heating can dry it out, so start checking early.
- Salt content differs between lemon-pepper blends. The label determines
  whether separate salt belongs in this recipe.
- Butter supplies richness; fresh lemon at the end adds brightness.
  Lemon juice is flavoring, not a substitute for cooking fish safely.

HANDLING AND LEFTOVERS
Keep raw fish separate from ready-to-eat food. Wash hands and clean tools
after handling it. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours, or within 1 hour
when the surrounding air is above 90 F (32 C).
