Scale the saucy batch

Choose how much boneless chicken you are grilling. The dry rub and the finishing BBQ sauce both scale with it. Reserve clean sauce for brushing at the end—never reuse sauce that touched raw chicken.

2 lb · about 4–6 thighs

Cook boneless chicken to 165°F in the thickest part. Sauce color and clear juices do not prove doneness.

For 2 lb boneless chicken thighs

Bring to the grill

Choose your burner plan

Start over direct heat for color, then move to the unlit zone before the sauce goes on. Keep a cooler zone ready: sugar burns quickly.

Burners 1–2 lit · sauce goes on over the cooler side.

1. Season the chicken and split the sauce

15–30 min

Season thighs with smoked paprika, garlic powder, onion powder, black pepper, kosher salt, and a pinch of cayenne. Stir ketchup, molasses, apple cider vinegar, brown sugar, Worcestershire, smoked paprika, and hot sauce. Put half in a clean bowl for serving and keep half for brushing.

Science: Separating sauce prevents cross-contamination and keeps a clean, glossy final layer.

2. Grill the chicken before saucing

400–425°F ambient

Follow the grill manufacturer’s lighting sequence and clean the grates. Sear thighs 3–4 minutes per side, then move them over the unlit zone and close the lid. Cook until the thickest piece is about 150–155°F.

3. Paint, turn, paint again

Final 4–6 min

Brush a thin layer of sauce over the chicken, close the lid for 1–2 minutes, turn, and brush again. Repeat once or twice over indirect heat. Move briefly over direct heat only if you need extra tack and char; watch constantly for flare-ups.

Science: Multiple thin coats build a lacquer. One thick coat burns on the outside before it sets.

4. Verify, rest, and flood the plate

165°F · 5 min rest

Probe the thickest thigh sideways and remove only at 165°F. Rest 5 minutes, then brush or spoon over the reserved clean sauce. Add pickles, toasted buns, or slaw.

Safety: The thermometer decides. Keep the clean finishing sauce separate from the raw-chicken brush.
