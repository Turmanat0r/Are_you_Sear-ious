Island heat · Gas grill

Jerk spice.
Turkey & pineapple.

A fragrant, peppery turkey tenderloin with charred pineapple salsa and a clean two-zone finish.

GRILL AMBIENT400–425°Findirect then direct
TURKEY INTERNAL165°Fthickest center
COOK ESTIMATE24–32 minfirst check at 20

Ingredients

1.25 lb turkey tenderloin

1 tbsp lime juice

1 tbsp neutral oil

2 tsp allspice

1 tsp thyme

1 tsp smoked paprika

½ tsp cinnamon

½ tsp black pepper

½ tsp kosher salt

1 cup pineapple, diced

¼ cup red onion and scallion

Equipment

Instant-read thermometer · tongs · rimmed tray · grill basket or foil for salsa

1. Make the jerk paste

Mix lime juice, oil, allspice, thyme, paprika, cinnamon, pepper, and salt. Rub over the turkey. Keep chilled while the grill heats. Toss pineapple, onion, scallion, and a pinch of salt separately.

Science: The thin paste seasons the surface without creating a wet coating that steams instead of browns.

2. Set up two-zone heat

Follow the grill manufacturer’s lighting sequence and stabilize 400–425°F at grate level. Light enough burners for the target, leaving an unlit zone for the turkey. Keep the lid closed between checks.

3. Cook indirect, then sear

Place turkey over the unlit zone. Turn once after 12–15 minutes. Grill pineapple in a basket or on foil over direct heat for 4–6 minutes, stirring once. When turkey reaches 155–160°F, move it over direct heat for 30–45 seconds per side.

Science: Indirect heat protects lean meat from scorching while the brief direct finish browns the spice paste. Sugar in pineapple and any added brown sugar can flare.

4. Verify, rest, and slice

Probe the thickest center from the side. Remove only at 165°F / 73.9°C. Rest 5 minutes, slice across the grain, and fold the charred pineapple salsa through with lime juice.

Safety: Turkey is poultry: use the 165°F endpoint, not the 145°F endpoint used for whole pork cuts.

Source basis: USDA FSIS safe-temperature guidance for poultry; original flavor and gas-grill adaptation by Are You Sear-ious.
