# Sear-iously Smothered Beef Ribs

Peppery, tender beef short ribs with a sticky brown sugar–Worcestershire BBQ glaze and plenty of warm sauce at the table.

Base batch: 4 lb / 1.81 kg raw bone-in English-cut short ribs, about 4 hearty servings. Choose individual ribs with 2–3 in / 5–7.5 cm of meat above the bone.

Prep: about 25 minutes. Cooking: plan 4–6 hours, plus 15–20 minutes resting. Extra-thick 3–4 in / 7.5–10 cm ribs may need 5–8 hours. These are estimates, not doneness tests. Ingredients scale with batch weight in index.html; cooking time does not.

## Ingredients

### Ribs and rub

- 4 lb / 1.81 kg thick English-cut bone-in beef short ribs
- 2 tbsp / 30 ml yellow mustard
- 12 g kosher salt, weighed
- 1 tbsp / about 7 g coarse black pepper
- 2 tsp / about 6 g garlic powder
- 1 tsp / about 2.5 g onion powder
- 2 tsp / about 4.6 g smoked paprika

### Shallow braise

- ¾ cup / 180 ml unsalted beef stock
- 1 tbsp / 15 ml apple cider vinegar
- Hot water as needed to maintain shallow liquid in the pan

### BBQ sauce

- 1½ cups / 360 ml ketchup
- ¼ cup / 55 g packed dark brown sugar
- 2 tbsp / 30 ml unsulphured molasses
- 3 tbsp / 45 ml Worcestershire sauce
- 3 tbsp / 45 ml apple cider vinegar
- ¼ cup / 60 ml water, plus a splash if needed
- 1 tsp / about 3 g garlic powder
- 1 tsp / about 2.3 g smoked paprika
- 1 tsp / about 2.3 g black pepper
- ⅛ tsp / about 0.25 g cayenne, optional
- 2 tbsp / 28 g unsalted butter

Sauce yield: approximately 2½ cups / 600 ml, depending on reduction. Split half for glazing and half for clean serving sauce. Metric volumes use practical kitchen approximations. Weigh salt, since teaspoon weights vary by brand and crystal size.

## Grill plan

Use an indirect zone on a gas grill with at least two main burners. Choose which burners you want lit, leaving enough space over unlit burners for the entire rib pan. Adjust your selected lit burners to hold 275–300°F / 135–149°C measured at grate level beside the food. Follow the grill manufacturer's lighting instructions and safe operating settings. No specific burner is chosen for you.

Use snug metal pans that fit entirely over the unlit zone. Do not crowd or stack ribs; larger batches need more pans or separate cooks. Have enough fuel for the long cook.

## Method

1. Thaw ribs in the refrigerator, pat dry, and trim hard surface fat and accessible silverskin. Keep meat attached to the bones. Apply a thin mustard film, mix the rub ingredients, and season all meaty sides. Cook now or refrigerate 4–24 hours. Do not repeat the salt after an overnight rest.

2. Cook bone-side down over indirect heat at 275–300°F / 135–149°C with the lid closed. Rotate positions if needed. Allow about 1½–2 hours for standard ribs, or 2–3 hours for extra-thick ribs, until deep brown and the rub no longer wipes off easily. Internal temperature may be around 160–175°F / 71–79°C, but a set crust is the cue to cover. Do not sauce yet.

3. Place ribs bone-side down in a snug metal pan in one layer. Pour stock and braising vinegar around the ribs, keeping their tops exposed. Aim for about ¼ in / 6 mm liquid underneath, adjusting with hot water for the pan. Cover tightly with foil and cook over the unlit zone at about 300°F / 149°C. Standard ribs may need 2–3 hours covered; extra-thick ones 2½–4 hours. Start probing after 90 minutes covered for standard ribs, or 2 hours for extra-thick ribs, then check every 20–30 minutes. Open foil away from your face. Add hot water if dry and reseal. Finish when a probe enters several spots with very little resistance, often around 200–205°F / 93–96°C, without touching bone. Keep cooking any ribs that still resist.

4. During the final 30–45 minutes of braising, combine all sauce ingredients except butter. Simmer gently for 8–12 minutes, stirring, until coating a spoon but still pourable. Remove from heat and whisk in butter. Loosen with a splash of water if needed. Split equally into brushing sauce and a separate clean serving portion. Keep the serving portion away from the brush; refrigerate if prepared in advance and warm before serving.

5. Transfer tender ribs carefully to a clean, lightly oiled metal rack over a shallow tray, or clean grates if they hold together. Keep over unlit burners at 275–300°F / 135–149°C. Leave the braising liquid behind. Brush tops and sides with a thin coat of brushing sauce, close the lid for 5–8 minutes, and repeat for roughly three coats total. Allow 15–25 minutes, stopping sooner if the edges darken rapidly. Avoid direct flames.

6. Rest loosely tented for 15–20 minutes. Warm the clean reserved sauce gently and spoon generously over the ribs. Pass the rest at the table. Use clean utensils and do not combine used brushing sauce with serving sauce.

## Why this works

Mustard holds the peppery rub in place. Refrigerated salting gives salt time to move beneath the surface. A dry first stage builds crust; a covered moist stage limits drying while collagen softens. Thin, late sauce layers thicken into a glaze. The separately reserved sauce gives the finished ribs their generous smothering.

## Temperature and storage

Whole-cut beef's safe minimum is 145°F / 63°C followed by at least a 3-minute rest. That is not the tenderness target for short ribs. Use the higher practical tenderness range and a probe test as described above.

Refrigerate leftovers in shallow containers within 2 hours, or 1 hour when air is above 90°F / 32°C. Reheat leftovers to 165°F / 74°C. Check ingredient labels for mustard, milk, fish, and wheat as applicable.
