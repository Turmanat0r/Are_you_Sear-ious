Choose your burner plan

Light the burners on one side, put the roast over the unlit side, and keep the lid closed. For the final sear, move the roast over direct heat and watch the herb crust.

Burners 2–3 lit · roast over the cool side · indirect heat first.

1. Salt early; build the Christmas crust

12–24 hr ahead

Pat the roast dry. Season all over with the measured kosher salt and leave uncovered on a rack in the refrigerator. Before grilling, mix black pepper, rosemary, thyme, juniper, garlic, orange zest, and olive oil into a rough paste; press it onto the roast, keeping the bone side lightly coated.

Science: Salt needs time to move inward and season the roast beyond the surface. The herb paste belongs later so its delicate aromatics do not dry out overnight.

2. Set the grill for a gentle roast

250–275°F ambient

Follow your grill manufacturer’s lighting sequence. Stabilize grate-level ambient temperature at 250–275°F with the roast over the unlit zone. Put the roast bone-side down in a rack over a rimmed pan. Insert the leave-in probe into the center, avoiding bone and large fat seams.

Ambient is not internal. The grill’s air temperature is the heat supply; the center probe is the decision point. Do not use time alone for a holiday roast.

3. Reverse-sear without rushing

First check at 60 min

Cook indirectly until the center is approaching your preferred serving temperature, then add a short buffer for the final sear and carryover. For the USDA safety endpoint, the roast must reach 145°F before serving. When ready, move it to a tray and turn all burners high enough to create a hot searing zone. Sear the crust 45–75 seconds per broad side, rotating to brown without burning the herbs.

Why reverse-sear? Low heat reduces the gray overcooked band. The final blast rebuilds the crust after the long gentle cook.

4. Rest, whisk, and carve

20–30 min rest

Transfer the roast to a board and tent loosely. Stir the scaled sour cream, prepared horseradish, Dijon, lemon juice, chives, and a pinch of salt. Taste and add more horseradish at the table. After the rest, carve between the bones or remove the bones first, then slice across the grain.

Safety versus preference: USDA lists 145°F for beef roasts with at least 3 minutes of rest. Serving a lower doneness is a personal preference with higher food-safety risk, not an equivalent endpoint.
